Introduction

In the AI Lab for AIML-500, I explored prompt engineering and AI tools through three activities: comparing one prompt across three large language models, working with a custom GPT tutor, and generating a researched article with STORM. I then used what I learned to design, build, and test my own AI/ML tutor bot in Mizou. It addresses a common problem for beginners: explanations pitched at the wrong level, with no way to check understanding.

Description

The AI/ML Tutor is a chatbot that teaches AI/ML basics one concept at a time. It greets the learner, says it is an AI that can make mistakes, and asks what topic they want and how much they already know. For a broad request, it offers subtopics. It then explains one concept in plain language with an example and asks one quiz question at a time. It adapts to the learner’s answers, getting harder after a correct answer and simpler after a wrong one. It closes with a summary, one thing to review, and a suggested next concept.

Objective

Beginners who want to learn AI/ML basics need clear explanations at their own level and a way to check their understanding. General chat tools often assume too much or too little and do not confirm that the learner understood. My goal was to build a tutor that explains first, quizzes second, and adapts to the learner, while staying honest about its limits. I also wanted to learn how prompt structure and wording change an AI’s output, and how to judge that output critically.

Process

I followed a design-thinking process.

  1. Groundwork. Comparing Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini showed me that thorough responses can break explicit constraints. Testing the Tutor Me GPT showed me what a tutor that waits for answers feels like. Checking STORM’s article claim by claim reinforced that AI output needs verification.
  2. Empathize. I identified learners’ pain points: explanations that are too advanced or too simple, no way to check understanding, confident but wrong answers, and tools that give answers instead of teaching. I wrote a persona: Maya, 28, who works full time, has no coding background, and has about 30 minutes a few evenings a week.
  3. Define and ideate. I brainstormed three ideas: a concept explainer, an explain-then-quiz tutor, and a jargon translator. I rated each on impact, feasibility, and user value, and chose the explain-then-quiz tutor because it directly addresses the lack of a way to check understanding.
  4. Prototype. I built the bot in Mizou’s Custom mode. I replaced the quiz template’s instructions with my own, condensed to fit the 1,000-character limit (987 used).
  5. Test, tune, iterate. I tested 13 scenarios, including vague topics, wrong answers, off-topic requests, homework requests, and personal information. I also ran three ethical checks: a nervous non-native-English learner, a stereotype-style question, and a medical question.

The known limitation: when a learner pastes the quiz question back, the bot reveals the answer. I tried two instruction fixes and documented the limitation rather than hiding it. The two partial passes were a wrong-answer follow-up that reused the same example, and a missing warning when the learner shared personal details.

Tips if you build a bot like this

  • Enter your instructions in the bot’s settings, not the chat box.
  • Write instructions to fit the character limit from the start.
  • Check that leftover template text doesn’t conflict with your instructions.
  • Test each rule with a specific scenario, including edge cases like a learner pasting the question back.

Value proposition

Unique value

This project demonstrates how I apply quality-engineering principles to AI by defining expected outcomes, validating AI responses against realistic learner scenarios, identifying gaps, and transparently documenting both successes and failures.

Relevance

This project connects directly to my quality engineering experience by applying scenario-based testing to AI-powered automation. By defining expected behavior, validating outputs, and identifying failure cases, I can help build more reliable AI solutions that improve testing efficiency and behave as expected.
